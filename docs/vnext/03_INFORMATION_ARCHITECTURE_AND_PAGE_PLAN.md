# YCN Labs vNext — Information Architecture & Full Site Plan

---

## 1. Navigation philosophy

Primary navigation must remain small, human and outcome-led.

### Recommended primary nav

- **Work**
- **What We Solve**
- **How We Work**
- **About**
- **Start a Project**

Optional secondary/utility links:
- Engineering
- Insights (only when real content exists)

Do not create a mega menu full of technologies or future capabilities.

---

## 2. Route map

### Core launch routes

| Route | Purpose | Motion intensity |
|---|---|---|
| `/` | Product-led cinematic homepage | High |
| `/what-we-solve` | Business problems/outcomes, not service cards | Medium |
| `/work` | Verified client/internal/concept work index | Medium |
| `/work/[slug]` | Case-study storytelling | High/Medium |
| `/how-we-work` | Clear engagement/process explanation | Medium |
| `/about` | YCN point of view, team/company facts | Low/Medium |
| `/engineering` | Technical depth for evaluators | Medium, more diagrams than spectacle |
| `/start-a-project` | Guided enquiry | Low, utility-first |
| `/thank-you` | Confirmation/recovery guidance | Low |
| `/privacy` | Legal | Minimal |
| `/terms` | Legal | Minimal |
| `/cookies` | Legal/cookie info if applicable | Minimal |
| `/404` | Helpful recovery | Low |

### Later only if supported

- `/insights`
- `/insights/[slug]`
- specific outcome landing pages when real demand/evidence justifies them;
- careers;
- dedicated product pages;
- additional languages.

## 3. Homepage role

Home answers:

1. What kind of change does YCN create?
2. What does that look like in a product?
3. What changes for the customer?
4. What changes for the business team?
5. How does YCN think/build?
6. What evidence can YCN show honestly?
7. How do I start?

Home does **not** attempt to list every capability.

---

## 4. What We Solve page

This page is outcome-led.

Suggested narrative areas:

### A. Be easier to understand
Examples:
- clearer digital presence;
- clearer offer;
- clearer choices;
- better information architecture.

### B. Make customer action easier
Examples:
- enquiries;
- booking;
- ordering;
- applications;
- requests;
- customer portals.

### C. Make the work behind it clearer
Examples:
- ownership;
- status;
- workflow;
- hand-off;
- follow-up;
- fewer manual steps.

### D. Build something new
Examples:
- digital product;
- internal product;
- SaaS/product concept;
- customer experience;
- tailored system.

Capabilities such as websites, web apps, mobile apps, e-commerce or automation can be mentioned **inside the relevant outcome**, not as the site's organizing principle.

Digital marketing must not be presented as an active proven service until the Claims & Proof register supports it.

---

## 5. Work page

Work must be evidence-led.

Each tile/item shows:
- project name;
- classification: Client Work / Internal Product / Concept Build;
- stage;
- one-line problem;
- one-line result/outcome if verified;
- visual;
- link to detail.

No fake logos.
No fake testimonials.
No generic "trusted by" wall.

If real client work is not yet publishable, say so honestly.

---

## 6. Case study page

Case-study structure:

1. Hero media / product in context.
2. The situation.
3. What needed to change.
4. Product experience.
5. Business-side flow/system.
6. Key design/engineering decisions.
7. Verified outcome/proof.
8. What changed after launch (only if known).
9. Technical appendix (optional).
10. Next-project CTA.

The case study should have its own visual protagonist, like Forge uses the vehicle. Do not reuse the homepage phone merely because it exists.

---

## 7. How We Work page

Plain language.

Recommended steps:
1. Understand
2. Shape
3. Prototype
4. Build
5. Validate
6. Launch
7. Improve

The exact number can change after delivery-process audit. Avoid promising fixed timelines unless evidence/operations support them.

Include:
- what the client needs to bring;
- what YCN needs;
- how decisions are made;
- how scope is controlled;
- how feedback works;
- how change requests work;
- what happens after launch.

---

## 8. About page

Purpose: explain YCN's point of view and verified company facts.

Possible sections:
- Why YCN exists.
- What YCN believes about useful software.
- What YCN chooses not to do.
- Founder/team facts that are verified.
- Operating principles.
- Location/remote model if factually supported.
- CTA.

Do not inflate company size, client history or awards.

---

## 9. Engineering page

This is where technical depth belongs.

Possible sections:
- Architecture approach.
- Performance.
- Accessibility.
- Security.
- Reliability.
- Testing.
- Observability.
- Deployment/operations.
- Stack examples **in context**, not a logo wall.
- How technology choices are made.
- Technical case-study notes.

A non-technical visitor should never have to visit this page to understand the homepage.

---

## 10. Start a Project page

The form begins with the problem, not project type.

First question:
> **What needs to work better?**

Possible fields:
- What is happening today?
- What do you want customers/users to be able to do?
- What does your team need to see/do afterward?
- Existing website/product link (optional).
- Timeline (optional).
- Budget band (optional unless Owner changes).
- Contact details.
- Preferred contact method.

Do not make "Website / App / E-commerce / Custom Software" the first required choice.

---

## 11. Footer

Keep broad and quiet.

Recommended groups:
- Work
- What We Solve
- How We Work
- About
- Engineering
- Start a Project
- Privacy
- Terms

Descriptor:
> **Software, engineered to hold up.**

---

## 12. Cross-page motion rule

Homepage can carry the strongest choreography.

Supporting pages should inherit:
- typography;
- transition grammar;
- nav behavior;
- color system;
- motion quality;

but use fewer cinematic pins. A site where every page behaves like the homepage becomes exhausting and slower.
