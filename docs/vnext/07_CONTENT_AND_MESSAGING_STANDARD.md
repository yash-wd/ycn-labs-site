# YCN Labs vNext — Content & Messaging Standard

---

## 1. Message hierarchy

Homepage first reading:

1. Business needs to improve.
2. YCN shapes a digital product around that need.
3. The product helps the customer act.
4. The product helps the business know what happens next.
5. YCN builds the underlying system deliberately.
6. Proof is shown honestly.
7. The visitor can start with the problem.

---

## 2. Positioning direction

Internal working statement:

> **YCN Labs designs and engineers digital products around real business needs.**

Do not force a public expansion of "YCN".

Strong retained descriptor:

> **Software, engineered to hold up.**

---

## 3. Working homepage hero copy

### Working H1
> **Build what moves the business forward.**

### Working support
> YCN Labs turns business problems into digital products that make it easier for customers to act, teams to work clearly, and the business to keep moving.

### CTA
> **Start a Project**

This is working production copy until Owner signs off final copy.

---

## 4. Outcome language

Prefer:
- Make the next step obvious.
- Make action simple.
- Make every request visible.
- Give the work somewhere to go.
- Make ownership clear.
- Reduce avoidable steps.
- Make progress visible.
- Build room for what comes next.

Avoid:
- Web Development Solutions
- Innovative Digital Transformation
- Leverage Cutting-Edge Technology
- Seamless End-to-End Ecosystem
- Unlock Growth Through Innovation
- Next-Gen Scalable Solutions

unless a phrase is made concrete immediately.

---

## 5. Services/capabilities

Capabilities can be described, but not as the homepage mental model.

Instead of:
> Web Development

Use:
> Help customers understand what you offer and take the next step.

Then deeper copy may say this can involve a website, commerce experience, portal, app or tailored system when true.

---

## 6. Technology names

Homepage:
- generally hidden.

What We Solve:
- only if directly relevant.

Case Study:
- optional technical appendix.

Engineering:
- appropriate place for stack/architecture detail.

Do not create a scrolling technology-logo wall.

---

## 7. Copy length for cinematic sections

Major motion screen:
- 3–9 word statement;
- optional 1–2 short supporting sentences.

Pinned chapter:
- one short headline;
- max ~40–60 words visible at once.

Do not make users read a 150-word paragraph while an object is moving.

---

## 8. Voice

YCN voice:
- clear;
- confident;
- mature;
- calm;
- specific;
- human;
- technically credible without jargon.

Avoid:
- hype;
- slangy agency voice;
- fake urgency;
- aggressive sales tone;
- empty superlatives.

---

## 9. Claims and proof

Never publish unverified:
- percentages;
- revenue growth;
- conversion increase;
- time saved;
- client count;
- uptime;
- certifications;
- accessibility conformance;
- performance scores;
- partner counts;
- awards;
- testimonials.

Results graphs must use:
- verified measured data, or
- an explicit "Illustrative" label with no implication that it is client proof.

---

## 10. Work labels

Every work item uses one:
- Client Work
- Internal Product
- Concept Build

Also show stage where relevant:
- Concept
- Prototype
- In Development
- Live
- Archived

Do not blur these statuses.

---

## 11. CTA language

Primary:
- Start a Project

Support:
- Tell us what needs to work better.
- You do not need a technical brief to start.

Avoid forcing visitor to choose "website / app / software" before explaining the problem.

---

## 12. FAQ tone

Answers should be direct and practical.

Bad:
> Our bespoke discovery methodology unlocks the optimal technological pathway...

Good:
> Start with the problem. We will work out what kind of product and technology make sense after we understand it.

### 13.7 FAQ Ratified Conservative Copy
Accurately reflects business terms without unverified commitments or informal marketing slogans:
1. **[OWNER-RATIFIED (2026-09-21)]**  
   *Q:* "I am not technical. Can I still start?"  
   *A:* "Yes. Start with the problem in your business. We work out what kind of website, tool, or software makes sense after we understand what happens today."
2. **[OWNER-RATIFIED (2026-09-21)]**  
   *Q:* "Do I need to know whether I need a website, an app, or a system?"  
   *A:* "No. Most business owners know what is slow, lost, or painful in their day-to-day work. Deciding the right format is our job."
3. **[OWNER-RATIFIED (2026-09-21)]**  
   *Q:* "Can YCN work with the tools and systems we already use?"  
   *A:* "Yes. We look at what is already working first. We keep, connect, or replace only what makes business sense."
4. **[OWNER-RATIFIED (2026-09-21)]**  
   *Q:* "I already have a website or software. Can you improve it?"  
   *A:* "Yes, if improving it is sensible and cost-effective. If rebuilding from scratch would save you money and headaches in the long run, we explain exactly why before starting."
5. **[OWNER-RATIFIED (2026-09-21)]**  
   *Q:* "How are cost and scope decided?"  
   *A:* "Scope, technical complexity, risk, and ongoing support determine price. Scope and pricing are agreed before work begins. Changes are discussed before they affect cost."  
   *~~"never through surprise invoices"~~ (Struck: informal marketing tone removed)*
6. **[OWNER-RATIFIED (2026-09-21)]**  
   *Q:* "Who owns the finished work?"  
   *A:* "Ownership and licensing terms are clearly specified in our written agreement before any work begins. You will always know exactly what code and assets belong to you."
7. **[OWNER-RATIFIED (2026-09-21)]**  
   *Q:* "What happens after launch?"
