# PART I — WHAT YCN LABS IS

## 1. Business identity

YCN Labs is not to be presented as a generic “digital agency” or a stack of trendy services.

The strongest business idea extracted from the supplied YCN discussions and current canonical project direction is:

> **Start with what is happening in the business. Build the appropriate digital connection around it.**

YCN may serve different kinds of work over time — websites, customer experiences, business systems, automation, software products, custom software, and later capabilities that are actually established and publishable — but the public experience should begin with the **business problem and customer outcome**, not a technology catalogue.

### North-star expression

**Business reality, engineered clarity.**

### Public comprehension standard

A non-technical proprietor, manager, founder, or customer should understand the point of YCN without knowing:

- React;
- Next.js;
- Three.js;
- PostgreSQL;
- cloud architecture;
- APIs;
- observability;
- security terminology.

Technical depth belongs deeper, especially on `/engineering`.

---

## 2. Audience hierarchy

### Primary early audience

Small and growing businesses, founders, proprietors and teams who know what is painful in their operation but may not know what technical solution they need.

Relevant business types discussed across the project include local retail, mobile-phone shops, salons, restaurants, service businesses, education businesses, logistics, real estate and other SMBs becoming more digital.

### Secondary audience

Startups and product owners who need a web, mobile, SaaS, workflow or custom software product.

### Technical / evaluation audience

Technical leads, experienced buyers, partners, CTOs, or procurement/evaluation stakeholders who want to inspect engineering quality, security boundaries, testing, reliability, performance and handover discipline.

### Reading-depth model

The same site should support:

**Glance → Scan → Inspect**

- **Glance:** one clear business statement and one recognizable visual.
- **Scan:** short outcomes, examples, process and capability.
- **Inspect:** deeper engineering evidence, implementation detail and limitations.

Do not create three different brand personalities.

## 3. Public full-form recommendation

**Recommended public brand: `YCN Labs`.**

Do not force an acronym expansion into the website.

The company is intended to grow beyond one narrow category. A forced expansion can make the brand smaller than the business.

### Internal semantic logic

The strongest internal motion / design mnemonic remains:

**You → Connected → Now**

translated visually as:

**request → connection → result**

This is useful as hidden design logic. It is **not** a public tagline or official expansion unless the Owner later changes D1/D2 explicitly.

**Your Connected Network**

This is the strongest conventional three-word expansion found in the earlier discussions, but it is weaker than keeping `YCN Labs` standalone because “Network” can imply networking/infrastructure.

# PART IV — REFERENCE-SITE STUDY

## 5. Forge Automotive — primary experience-quality benchmark

**Role for YCN:** editing discipline, cinematic rhythm, image-first storytelling, scroll-led progression, big-type turning points.

### Independently re-verified public evidence

Direct live fetch of `forgeautomotive.co.uk` was not available in this research environment.

Current public GSAP/showcase evidence associates Forge Automotive with:

- ScrollTrigger;
- Observer;
- SplitText;
- CustomEase;
- `useGSAP`;
- React;
- WebGL;
- Next.js;
- reduced-motion support.

Mindsparkle also lists Forge under Web Development / WebGL / GSAP / Interactive Design.

### Prior challenge-seat live-DOM observation

The earlier review-seat document reported this live-page rhythm:

**ten-image collage → Hero → tall portrait statement → logo marquee → numbered 01/03 sequence → vertical cinematic frame → six-row service list with swapping image → big-type turning moment → two quieter feature blocks → final line + CTA**

It also reported:

- one job per screen;
- short Hero copy;
- a repeated project CTA;
- image-first composition;
- lists rather than generic card grids;
- a deliberate quiet ending;
- small opening thumbnails with larger media staged later.

Treat that structural report as **prior-seat evidence**, not a newly re-rendered visual inspection.

### Why the experience feels premium

The transferable idea is not “use WebGL.”

It is:

- the media has a clear subject;
- each viewport has one dominant thought;
- typography and image behave as one composition;
- motion changes meaning;
- the visitor is given moments of silence;
- the visual climax is earned rather than continuous.

### YCN translation

Copy:

- editorial rhythm;
- image first;
- one principal action;
- one big turning-point scene;
- list-based presentation where cards would feel generic;
- intentional quiet after a climax;
- media staging.

Do **not** copy:

- Forge branding;
- automotive identity;
- assets;
- layouts;
- source code;
- claims;
- proof devices such as client/brand/logo collections unless YCN has equivalent evidence.

**Confidence:** medium. Stack/showcase evidence is current; direct live rendering was unavailable in this session. Structural rhythm comes from the supplied prior-seat live-DOM report.

## 9. Framer — rich experience without loading everything at once

**Role for YCN:** performance-under-weight benchmark and editing/control reference.

The current Framer homepage is visually dense and product-demonstration heavy, yet Framer’s current official documentation describes performance techniques including:

- responsive image variants;
- `srcset` / `sizes`;
- automatic WebP/AVIF delivery;
- below-fold image lazy loading;
- poster-first video behavior;
- video loading near the viewport;
- pausing certain looping video when out of view;
- pre-rendering / caching strategies;
- image resizing per device;
- staged media delivery.

### YCN lesson

**Load less initially; do not automatically design less overall.**

YCN can be cinematic if delivery is disciplined:

- semantic Hero immediately available;
- correctly sized priority Hero image;
- below-fold media lazy;
- video poster first;
- video mounted only when needed;
- animations paused offscreen;
- desktop-only Scene 5 Three.js lazy;
- phones never request Three.js.

Do not copy Framer’s product UI or marketing identity.

**Confidence:** high; current homepage text and official current performance/image/video documentation were retrieved.

# PART VI — YCN EXPERIENCE IDEA

## 15. North-star art direction

> **Business reality, engineered clarity.**

Four visual layers:

### A. Real business

Believable environment, people, customer need and operational friction.

### B. Digital experience

Recognizable customer-facing actions: enquiry, booking, order, portal, request.

### C. Engineering system

Shown only when useful: routing, ownership, state, connection, recovery, testing.

### D. Premium restraint

Strong typography, negative space, photography, precise motion and confident pauses.

### Senior-design rule

> **One screen. One idea. One meaningful movement.**

The final site must not resemble a generic generated SaaS / “AI agency” template.

Avoid:

- glowing orbs with no meaning;
- purple/rainbow fog;
- abstract blobs;
- glass cards everywhere;
- neon tubes;
- particle fields;
- random 3D;
- fake terminals;
- generated dashboard screenshots presented as shipped software;
- every section fading up the same way;

### AI-scent test

If a section could be pasted into 100 other software/agency sites without changing its meaning, it is not YCN enough.

# PART VII — HOMEPAGE STORYBOARD

## 17. Story spine

**Recognition → Friction → Failure → Discovery → Connection → Resolution → Confidence → Action**

Do not turn this into eight isolated landing-page sections.

---

## 18. Scene 0 — Continuum entrance

### Job

Introduce the YCN connection idea without delaying access to the page.

### Feel

Designed, precise, short.

### Rules

- Hero exists and is usable underneath from frame one.
- Entrance can be skipped.
- Escape skips.
- focus is handed into the page after dismissal.
- reduced motion gets a short static logo treatment.
- official logo only.
- no public Y/C/N explanation.
- no loader aesthetic.
- no particles / sci-fi / code rain.

---

## 19. Scene 1 — Hero

### Canonical job

Immediate understanding.

### Composition

- full-viewport or near-full-viewport editorial field;
- one strong image/environment;
- canonical H1 dominant;
- concise support;
- one principal CTA;
- secondary route visually quieter;
- generous negative space.

### Motion

- restrained image drift / mask / crop evolution;
- headline enters with editorial control, not a default stagger preset;
- no effect should delay comprehension.

### Success test

A proprietor should understand within seconds that YCN builds useful websites/software around the business.

---

## 20. Scene 2 — Friction

### Job

Recognition.

### Visual

Same world; information begins to fragment:

- calls;
- messages;
- notes;
- spreadsheets;
- manual follow-up;
- disconnected tools.

### Motion

Objects separate or drift only enough to show fragmentation.

No “exploding UI.”

---

## 21. Scene 3 — Failure

### Job

Create the memorable absence.

### Visual

Same customer and same request. The customer believes the request was sent. The business has no owned next step.

### Tone

Cooler, quieter, restrained.

### Non-negotiable

The tension comes from **nothing happening**, not glitch effects.

### Trust rule

Fictional scenario labels remain visible. Never imply this is a real YCN client case.

---

## 22. Scene 4 — Route / YCN enters

### Job

Turn from problem to possibility.

### Composition

Use the canonical pathways, but avoid four identical generic cards if a more editorial/list/spatial treatment communicates them clearly.

### Motion

The composition becomes more organized. The visitor should feel the route becoming legible.

---

## 23. Scene 5 — System climax

### Job

Show what “connection” actually means.

The main character is the request.

### Desktop

This remains the one scene where spatial depth / lazy Three.js is allowed under the final architecture.

The visitor should understand:

**CUSTOMER → EXPERIENCE → BUSINESS WORKFLOW → RESULT**

not:

**“look at this 3D demo.”**

### Mobile

No Three.js.

Use DOM/SVG, stacked tiers, a clear travelling signal and native scrolling.

### Pause test

Freeze the scene at representative moments. The frame should still explain where the request is and what happens next.

---

## 24. Scene 6 — Same moment, resolved

### Job

Pay off Scene 3.

### Non-negotiable

Match Scene 3 as closely as practical:

- same people;
- same request;
- same environment;
- same framing logic;
- same geometry.

Change only the outcome.

### Tone

Slightly warmer, not celebratory.

---

## 25. Scene 7 — Broaden and close

### Job

Show that the enquiry story was one example of a broader YCN capability.

### Composition

Return to calm:

- broader capability routes;
- honest work/proof where allowed;
- one closing statement;
- one principal CTA;
- strong negative space.

Do not finish with another services dump.

# PART VIII — COPY SYSTEM

## 26. Copy rules

In addition to the canonical Content Spec:

- lead with the customer / owner situation;
- one idea per line;
- display statements usually 3–7 words;
- support usually one short sentence;
- verbs over nouns;
- do not describe YCN with adjectives like “premium,” “trusted,” or “expert”;
- no unsupported absolutes;
- no response-time promises without register approval;
- no technology-stack language on the homepage;
- if a line could belong to any agency, cut it;
- candidate copy never becomes canon without Owner ratification + Content Spec amendment.

# PART X — MOTION LANGUAGE

## 33. Motion principle

Every motion should answer:

> **What changed, and what should the visitor understand?**

Allowed semantic meanings:

- entering;
- separating;
- stalling;
- connecting;
- progressing;
- receiving;
- settling.

Do not animate because the skill/library exists.

---

## 34. Motion vocabulary

### Typography

- line/word reveals only for major editorial moments;
- avoid identical reveal treatment everywhere;
- preserve readable static state.

### Photography

- restrained parallax;
- crop/mask change;
- depth shift;
- matched Scene 3 → Scene 6 transition.

### Interface overlays

- enter as state changes;
- never become a floating HUD;
- A live micro-signal that the system is real — YCN's is the enquiry object #YCN-8041 moving from 'Sent · Waiting for a reply' to 'Received · Assigned · Reply sent', always beside an illustrative label.

### Signal

- cyan only when actual information/connection is moving;
- no decorative signal noise.

### Scene 5

- one controlled spatial climax;
- one primary desktop pin;
- deterministic entry/exit;
- clean disposal.

### Mobile

- native scroll;
- vertical composition;
- no WebGL;
- avoid long pinned sequences;
- motion must remain touch-friendly.

---

## 35. Timing feel

Use the existing design-system ranges as guidance:

- micro feedback: ~120–180ms;
- ordinary UI: ~240–360ms;
- scene reveals: roughly 300–800ms depending on meaning;
- cinematic holds/matched transitions: rare and deliberate;
- entrance short and non-blocking.

The user should never be forced to wait for animation before using the page.

---

## 36. What “smooth” means

Smoothness is not just FPS.

It means:

- no surprise layout shifts;
- no hitch when heavy media mounts;
- no scroll-jacking;
- no fighting native scroll;
- no visual jump when Scene 5 enters/exits;
- offscreen animation work stops;
- mobile stays responsive while media loads;
- reduced motion remains complete;
- the page still reads correctly when paused at any point.

---

# PART XI — PERFORMANCE UNDER RICH MEDIA

## 37. Philosophy

> **Load less initially, not automatically design less overall.**

The goal is not to copy Framer’s implementation. The goal is the same discipline: stage expensive work.

---

## 38. Initial viewport

Prioritize:

- semantic HTML;
- canonical Hero text;
- navigation;
- one correctly sized Hero media asset;
- only the CSS/JS required for the initial interaction.

Avoid:

- eager-loading every scene image;
- eager video download;
- homepage-wide WebGL;
- large animation libraries/plugins not used above fold.

---

## 39. Images

Use:

- responsive widths;
- accurate `srcset` / `sizes`;
- AVIF where supported;
- WebP fallback;
- separate mobile crops/compositions;
- below-fold lazy loading;
- fixed aspect ratios / reserved dimensions to avoid layout shift.

The Hero image is a priority asset; below-fold photography is not.
Hero is an <img> with fetchpriority="high", decoding="async" and declared dimensions; never a background-image.

Any file-size budget is an **engineering target to validate**, not a public claim.

---

## 40. Video

If video is later introduced:

- poster first;
- load/mount near the viewport;
- compress before upload;
- decorative autoplay muted/playsinline;
- pause when offscreen;
- avoid heavy above-fold autoplay;
- respect Save-Data / reduced-motion strategy.

---

## 41. JavaScript

- GSAP remains the primary choreography engine for the current homepage.
- Avoid loading multiple animation systems for the same job.
- Scene 5 Three.js stays section-local and desktop-capability-gated.
- Phones never fetch Three.js.
- Use IntersectionObserver / route-level/code-splitting where appropriate.
- dispose heavy contexts cleanly.

Framer Motion, if present at all, should be limited to a specific isolated micro-interaction that genuinely benefits from it rather than duplicating GSAP responsibilities.

---

## 42. Rendering / CSS

Prefer:

- transform;
- opacity;
- SVG stroke work;
- composited motion.

Avoid animating:

- large blurs;
- expensive shadows;
- layout-heavy properties;
- giant filters;
- unnecessary fixed backgrounds.

`content-visibility:auto` never on pinned or observer-measured sections (Architecture v2 §5).

---

## 43. Fonts

- maximum two families per the design system;
- only required weights;
- WOFF2/subsetting where appropriate;
- visible fallback behavior;
- font loading should not delay the first meaningful frame.

---

## 44. Measurement discipline

Internal targets are not verified outcomes.

Examples:

- mobile LCP `<2.5s` may be an internal target;
- performance budgets may be internal targets;
- no FPS claim without a named measurement method;
- no Lighthouse/CWV/WCAG publication until the Claims Register allows it.

Measure after implementation on representative devices/networks.

# PART XII — SUPPORTING-PAGE DIRECTION

## 45. Supporting pages should be calmer than the homepage

The homepage carries the cinematic story.

Supporting pages should feel like the same company, not repeat the whole show.

### What We Build

Use problem/outcome-led sections, strong visual hierarchy, a small number of meaningful illustrations or photos, and minimal card repetition.

### Work

Evidence-led, honest classification, limitations visible, no invented client logos or performance claims.

### How We Work

Editorial process narrative with clear progression and enough whitespace to make the process easy to understand.

### Engineering

More technical depth is allowed, but still clearly written. Separate practices, actual evidence and internal targets.

### About

Human, restrained, factual. Do not invent history, team size, awards, offices, partnerships or client counts.

### Start a Project

Calmest page in the system. Form clarity, validation, failure states and durable acceptance matter more than spectacle.

# PART XIII — SENIOR-DEVELOPER FINISH STANDARD

## 46. What makes the site feel deliberately built

### Design coherence

- one grid logic;
- deliberate type scale;
- consistent spacing;
- controlled radii;
- no random component styles;
- still frames look composed;
- responsive layouts are separately designed, not shrunk desktop.

### Motion coherence

- animation has semantic purpose;
- scenes have distinct but related movement;
- no universal fade-up preset;
- desktop and mobile choreography are intentionally different;
- reduced-motion parity is real.

### Engineering coherence

- lazy loading is intentional;
- heavy assets are scoped;
- no duplicate animation engines for the same behavior;
- no horizontal overflow;
- no hydration/layout surprises;
- accessibility survives the visual pass;
- error/fallback states remain usable;
- performance evidence is real.

### Copy coherence

- short;
- plain;
- business-first;
- canonical copy preserved;
- candidate copy clearly marked;
- no claims drift.

### QA coherence

- test 375 / 768 / 1024 / 1440;
- light/dark behavior where supported;
- keyboard;
- touch;
- reduced motion;
- Save-Data / weak network behavior where relevant;
- scene entry/exit;
- resize;
- back/forward navigation;
- no generated “evidence.”

# PART XIV — CONTENT DENSITY

## 47. One job per screen

A viewport may have:

- one dominant idea;
- one supporting explanation;
- one principal action;
- one principal visual behavior.

It should not simultaneously contain:

- giant title;
- paragraph;
- four cards;
- six icons;
- animated background;
- 3D object;
- metrics;
- testimonial;
- two CTAs;
- ticker.

That is how “premium” becomes template clutter.

---

## 48. Repetition that builds memory

Repetition is useful when it creates continuity:

- `Start a Project`;
- the request object;
- the same customer/business;
- the signal only when information is moving;
- Scene 3/6 matched geometry.

Do not repeat layouts simply because a component already exists.

# FINAL NORTH STAR

YCN should not become “a website with many cool effects.”

It should feel like experienced designers and software engineers understood a real business problem, edited the message down to what matters, and built an experience where every visual and technical decision has a reason.

The recurring test is:

> **Show something recognizable → expose the friction → let the failure be felt → reveal the route → show the request travel → resolve the same moment → broaden calmly → ask for one action.**

And the production standard is:

> **One screen. One idea. One meaningful movement.**

That is how YCN can feel cinematic without feeling generated, technically ambitious without feeling like a demo, and premium without pretending to have proof it does not yet have.
