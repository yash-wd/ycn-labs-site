# YCN Labs — Content depth & growth implementation brief
**16 Nov 2026 · Owner-review candidate · NOT an adopted website policy**

## Evidence and approach

Reviewed the live-indexed PWS Coding homepage, services, portfolio, About, contact and a published blog article, alongside YCN's **actual** main-branch inner-page source (What We Solve, What We Build, Work, How We Work, Engineering, About, Contact, existing Content Specification). PWS Coding is *research inspiration*, not a source of text or assets to copy. Its standout structures are owner-focused business situations, genuine project explanations, a clear account of what happens after contacting the builder and deeper specialized pages. Its indexed public copy also contains varied response-time/timeline claims; do not import them. Static search retrieval did **not** verify actual timing or behavior of interactive popups; do not assert that it did.

**YCN's new principle:** Each page must answer a *different* question. Homepage creates interest; inner pages help visitors make decisions. Blog teaches; gallery demonstrates; updates provide evidence of actual progress. Every conversion route must lead to a useful next step without false urgency.

## Page-specific copy proposals (not yet published)

### `/` — compact invitation, not the whole brochure
- **Hero candidate:** “Websites that earn trust. Software that saves time.”
- **Lead:** “We design websites, useful web apps and practical automations for businesses that want customers to find answers and teams to spend less time chasing information.”
- **Main action:** `Start a project`. Secondary: `See what we build`.
- **Interactive demonstration:** Three distinct, realistic code-built product scenes. The WEBSITE shows an actual customer journey; WEB APP shows a status being updated; AUTOMATION shows a replayable workflow. Scene states must respond to user input, not just tab switches. Every scene says `CONCEPT — NOT CLIENT WORK`.
- **Trust sentence:** “Know who is doing the work, what the next step is and what your agreement covers.” (No guarantee of 24/7 service, always-available founder, indefinite support or blanket IP transfer.)
- **What not to do:** Add a second product-demo gallery, long industry list, auto-popup, false user count or an animation that hides the offer.

### `/what-we-solve` — diagnostic clinic, NOT a duplicate services list
- **H1:** “Which part of the business is harder than it should be?”
- **Lead:** “Pick a situation that feels familiar. We’ll show a sensible first step—and when a simple change is better than custom software.”
- **Scenario 1: Customers ask the same questions.** Show a service page with location, hours, what is included and a direct action. **When a website edit is enough:** the answers already exist, but are hard to find. **When software may help:** the answer depends on availability, stock or real-time booking data.
- **Scenario 2: Requests arrive in too many places.** Show one request card with owner, status, last action and next step. **Start small:** a shared list with agreed statuses. **Build later:** when multiple staff need roles, audit history or connected systems.
- **Scenario 3: Staff copy the same details twice.** Interactive `form → record → notify` with a toggle showing the manual steps it replaces. **Caution:** real provider permissions and failure recovery are part of integration scope.
- **Scenario 4: The site looks fine, but visitors leave.** Show a side-by-side clarity diagnosis—first-line offer, phone navigation and contact path—without inventing conversion-rate numbers.
- **Contextual helper:** click `Which of these sounds like you?` to reveal one small three-choice guide. NEVER force an interrupting modal or capture an email just to see an answer.
- **Final CTA:** “Tell us the step that keeps getting lost.”

### `/what-we-build` — service depth and deliverables
- **H1:** “Different jobs need different digital tools.”
- **Lead:** “A website helps people understand your business. A web app helps people work. Automation helps a repeatable step happen with less manual effort.”
- **Websites:** Show example page anatomy: offer, trust information, service details, FAQs, mobile contact action. **Typical deliverables to discuss**: pages, CMS/update approach, responsive design, contact routing, performance/accessibility checks, analytics choice and handover. Do not promise all items for every contract.
- **Web applications:** Show a realistic request queue; explain account permissions, records, statuses, errors and reporting without using jargon as the main story. **Questions before scope**: who uses it, what record changes, what is private, what needs importing.
- **Automation & integrations:** Show inputs/outputs, approval points, who is notified, how retries work and manual fallback. **Avoid:** claiming automation works across every third-party app or WhatsApp API without provider review.
- **Maintenance:** What may be covered in a support plan—updates, backups, monitoring, changes, response expectations—only after a signed scope. Not an unlimited support guarantee.
- **Unique module:** “A page, a shared sheet, or custom software?” a three-column decision guide with realistic constraints, not a pricing table of guesses.
- **CTA:** “Let's choose the smallest useful first version.”

### `/work` — thoughtful evidence, not a gallery of fake success stories
- **H1:** “Look inside the decisions behind the screen.”
- **Lead:** “Here are concept interfaces we can explain and demonstrate. They are not passed off as client results.”
- **Case-study anatomy:** Situation → What a person needs to do → Interactive interface → Data handling/failure mode → Trade-offs → What we would test with a real client → Exact concept/client status.
- **First genuine internal case study:** “Building YCN Labs: making a simple enquiry reliable.” Show the design intent, form submission validation, saved-before-confirmed behavior, test screenshots and verification status. Distinguish tested staging from live production. Never claim business growth before launch.
- **Second original demo:** A request-tracking workspace with role-based interactions (concept, no real clients). Explain why one screen is useful and what it intentionally does *not* automate.
- **Asset request:** Owner to supply authentic screenshots, recordings or approve faithful code screenshots of the actual implemented YCN site. Don't fabricate case-study photos.
- **CTA:** “Have a situation like this? Describe it.”

### `/how-we-work` — anxiety-removing, concrete engagement page
- **H1:** “From the first conversation to a handover you can understand.”
- **Lead:** “You should know what we're building, when you'll review it and what you receive before the work begins.”
- **01 Listen:** The client describes the problem and current process. **You provide:** existing materials, key users and a decision-maker. **We propose:** the smallest useful first scope.
- **02 Define:** Written scope, dependencies, exclusions, milestones and price *as agreed for the project*. **Your decision:** approve or ask to change the proposed scope.
- **03 Show:** A usable preview with specific questions to answer; keep test and sample data separate from real customer data. **Your role:** try the main tasks and report problems.
- **04 Deliver:** Acceptance against the agreement, operational handover, access inventory, deployment instructions and any separately agreed maintenance. **Not a claim:** unlimited revisions or ownership of third-party components.
- **Useful visual:** a four-stage timeline with a sample deliverable at each step, not four identical text boxes. Click a step to see its sample document (clearly illustrative).
- **Contextual panel:** `What if the scope changes?` explains written change agreement, not a promise that scope is free to change.
- **CTA:** “Let's put the work in writing.”

### `/engineering` — evidence, not a checklist of fashionable tools
- **H1:** “What happens when something fails matters as much as the happy path.”
- **Lead:** “We focus on what your users experience and what your business needs to recover when something goes wrong.”
- **Real code-grounded study:** YCN enquiry flow: input validation → rate limiting → database save → notification → confirmation. Show accurate failure states: database down gets no false receipt; failed email leaves lead recoverable. Verify all claims against current code/staging tests.
- **Other chapters:** Accessibility before animation; performance budgets; avoiding surprise subscription costs; backups and restore drills; safe account ownership; clear logs without unnecessary personal data. Only claim verified operational practices, not universal compliance.
- **Interactive graphic:** User-triggered `Normal / Email fails / Database fails` schematic; explicitly a simulation.
- **CTA:** “Want to see how we'd approach your system?”

### `/about` — founder-led credibility, not corporate filler
- **H1:** “The work has someone accountable behind it.”
- **Lead:** “YCN Labs is an independent software and web studio built around clear conversations, useful design and maintainable delivery.”
- **Authentic founder section:** use Owner-confirmed name, role, location, genuine portrait and *verified* personal background. Do not invent years of experience, previous employers, team size or qualifications.
- **Editorial angle:** What kinds of confusing software or websites do you want to help businesses avoid? How do you make decisions about what *not* to build? Why do written scopes and a comprehensible handover matter to you?
- **Evidence:** one short original screen recording of a real implemented YCN feature, with a plain explanation. Actual face photo is an Owner-supplied asset, not AI generated.
- **CTA:** “Tell me what you're trying to make easier.”

### `/contact` — gentle, low-friction final step
- **H1:** “What's difficult in your business right now?”
- **Lead:** “A few lines in your own words are enough. Tell us the process or website you're trying to improve.”
- **Field hint:** “For example: requests arrive on WhatsApp and nobody knows who is following up.” Only one illustrative example, not a forced template.
- **Before-submit context panel:** `What happens after I send this?` → “Your enquiry is checked and saved. We review it and reply through the available contact route. We'll discuss scope before making commitments.” No invented reply SLA.
- **After-save:** show the actual reference number received from API; if storage fails, say no enquiry was saved and preserve typed text.
- **Alternative routes:** approved phone, WhatsApp and email, privacy link. No unsolicited marketing consent or extra tracking without decision.
- **Do not change the backend as part of copy/UX work.**

### `/gallery` — educational video and media library
- Hero: “See the idea. Understand how it works.”
- Sections: Website breakdowns, Software in action, Inside YCN, Educational short clips.
- Each verified video requires: real title, summary written for this site, original thumbnail, category, platform, exact YouTube/Instagram URL, duration if verified, a related article, copyright/consent proof and publish permission.
- Initial UX: fast thumbnail, useful written summary, `Watch the full video on YouTube/Instagram` external link with new-tab notice. Avoid automatic embeds and tracking. Optional later: click-to-load privacy-enhanced YouTube embed with accurate privacy notice; no Instagram embed by default.
- A gallery category with zero approved videos should not show fake thumbnails. The example package currently has **zero real media** and a coherent honest empty state. Publish/show in navigation only after at least one real video is approved.

### `/updates` — verified studio progress, publicity, promotions, events
- Hero: “The work, as it happens.”
- Only these categories: Milestone, Studio note, Press, Event, Promotion. Each item must have evidence and explicit Owner approval. Do not label internal implementation drafts as customer success.
- Publicity: link to the actual third-party mention, dated and attributed; if none exists, show none.
- Achievements: cite verifiable released projects, events, training, partnerships or testimonials (with permission). Do not invent awards, clients, reviews or percentages.
- Offers: visible terms, dates and eligibility approved by Owner; never imply fake countdown or scarcity.

## Contextual help, without annoying visitors

**What can be learned from PWS Coding:** The static site clearly has specific customer situations, direct contact routes and detailed service examples. The research did not verify popup timing or trigger mechanics. YCN should NOT copy its unverified popup patterns.

Build three original, *user-triggered* contextual assistance patterns, each with a genuine answer and useful next step:

1. `/what-we-solve`, next to the scenario selector: **“Not sure what kind of solution you need?”** Opens a small accessible inline decision panel. Choices: help customers find you, organize staff work, connect repeatable steps. Returns a relevant content link; does not ask for an email.
2. `/how-we-work`, near the scope step: **“What happens if we change the plan?”** Explains scope changes in plain language and points to written agreement; no blanket guarantee.
3. `/contact`, next to submit: **“What happens after I send this?”** Explains saved/enquiry receipt and how contact works, respecting actual form behavior.

Use native `<details>` where practical, or a properly implemented accessible popover after proven need. No modal on load, no exit-intent interception, no pressure timer, no dark-pattern urgency. Animations reveal context, not hide the service.

## SEO, accessibility and reputation controls

- Align words on `/blog` pages with real owner questions; avoid generic AI keyword pages or dozens of thin city-specific duplicates.
- Once approved and published: unique metadata, canonical link, sitemap inclusion, author identity, last-reviewed date based on actual review, relevant internal links and evidence where factual claims require it.
- For videos, Google provides video indexing guidance; a gallery of multiple video cards is generally NOT a dedicated single-video watch page. Add real structured data or watch pages only when warranted and based on verified info (video, thumbnail, date and URL).
- External link opens on YouTube/Instagram; no autoplay, no fabricated view counts, no surprise third-party embeds. Actual videos on the site should satisfy the relevant privacy notice and publishing rights.
- Core pages work with keyboard, high zoom, small phones and reduced-motion; use images and animation to explain, never to block reading or create false proof.
- Growth measurement: use a privacy-approved, minimal scorecard—search impressions and relevant queries (once Search Console is enabled), article-to-service navigation, genuine enquiries, and enquiries that become actual discussions. No broad analytics installation without Owner privacy approval.

## Sequenced implementation — 4 bounded workstreams, no new master plan

**Workstream 1 (existing):** Preserve and complete real R2 prototype; remove unsupported demo claims, make 3 scenes genuinely interactive, update active-route tests, obtain Owner visual acceptance. **This is still the top priority.**

**Workstream 2 (content depth):** Unique inner-page content for What We Solve, What We Build, Work, How We Work, About, Engineering and Contact. Start with What We Solve and Work—highest duplication and proof issues. Update existing routes, don't add alternate pages with the same purpose.

**Workstream 3 (editorial code):** AGY reviews this small Next.js content kit in an isolated branch; add `/blog`, `/blog/[slug]`, `/gallery`, `/updates` templates without changing the contact API. Start drafts in preview mode only. No new CMS/database; editorial data in TypeScript to begin with.

**Workstream 4 (real publication):** Owner accepts two substantial original articles, the first useful video, actual founder photo and proof-backed studio updates; then explicitly approve the page visibility, navigation, indexing and production release. Extra pages must not delay the essential enquiry-site release if real content is not ready.

**Boundaries:** Candidate A is a design direction, not automatically fully adopted. Preserve official logo; no fake client results. Existing GitHub protected-change protocol still applies. No branch push/merge/deploy under this document by itself.
